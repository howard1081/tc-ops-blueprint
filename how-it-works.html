How It Works — In Plain English

No technical background needed. This page explains what we're building, walks one deal through the system start to finish, and shows what your day looks like.

The Big Idea (30 seconds)

Imagine a TC office with eight employees. Each one has exactly one job — one reads contracts, one watches deadlines, one sends emails, and so on. In the middle of the office is a giant whiteboard where every deal is written down: address, price, dates, what's done, what's next.

The employees never talk to each other. They just read the whiteboard, do their one job, and write the result back on the whiteboard. When the board says a step is finished, the next employee sees it and starts theirs.

In our system, the "employees" are small computer programs (we call them workers), and the whiteboard is a shared online database (a smart spreadsheet called Airtable). One special worker — the Manager — checks work against a written rulebook, exactly like a supervisor with a checklist. Anything the rulebook doesn't cover, or anything sensitive, goes to a real person. The system does the busywork; people make the judgment calls.

One Deal, Start to Finish: 123 Maple Street

Day 1 — The contract arrives

  • The agent emails the signed contract to the system's inbox — that's all they have to do.
  • The Reader worker opens it and copies everything onto the whiteboard: buyer, seller, $329,000 price, $3,000 earnest money, closing date, inspection deadline — every blank on the contract.
  • STOP — a person checks first. Before anything else happens, a human compares what the Reader wrote down against the actual contract. Why? If a date is copied wrong, every reminder after it is wrong too. The person clicks Verified — that click unlocks everything else.
  • The moment it's verified: the deadline calendar is built, "we've opened your file" emails go out to everyone, the SkySlope file is created, and the document checklist appears (all 50+ items this deal will need).

Days 3–5 — Money and lender

  • The Calendar worker sees the earnest-money deadline coming and tells the Email worker: "chase the receipt."
  • The Email worker sends a friendly, branded reminder (from a pre-approved template — it never writes its own emails) and logs a copy.
  • The receipt comes back, the Filing worker recognizes it, checks it off the list, and files it in SkySlope. The whiteboard now shows: earnest money ✓.

Days 6–14 — Inspection and repairs

  • The buyer's inspection finds a leaky water heater. The buyer wants it fixed.
  • The Repairs worker drafts the repair request onto the official Tennessee form (RF654) — but it cannot send it.
  • The request goes to the Manager, who checks the rulebook. The agent's written rule says: "repairs under $2,500 that appear in the inspection report — approve." The water heater qualifies. Approved, stamped "approved by rule 4," and sent to the other agent.
  • If the buyer had instead demanded a new roof — no rule covers that — the Manager stops and texts the human: "123 Maple St: repair request needs your review. Tap here." Nothing moves until a person decides.
  • Meanwhile the client automatically gets a friendly update email with everything happening on their deal — plus the local utility phone numbers for their move.

Days 15–25 — Getting ready to close

  • The Calendar worker notices the lender has gone quiet → the Email worker checks in about the appraisal.
  • The client picks a closing time; the Closing worker books it with the title company (or closing attorney — the system knows which one this county uses).
  • The settlement statement arrives. The Manager checks the math against the contract; a person gives the final sign-off before the client ever sees a number.
  • Wire instructions are NEVER handled by the system. A person verifies them by phone, every time, no exceptions. This is the #1 fraud risk in real estate and it stays human forever.

Days 26–30 — Closing and after

  • Final signed documents are requested from title, uploaded to SkySlope, and the checklist is confirmed 100% complete for the broker.
  • The client gets a congratulations email with a review/referral ask. The CRM is updated. The file is archived so it can be produced within 24 hours for 3 years — exactly what Tennessee requires.

What Your Day Looks Like (the human's job)

You do not watch the system all day. The system comes to you:

WhenWhat you seeWhat you do
New contract arrivesA "verify this contract data" item with the contract and the copied data side by sideCompare, fix anything wrong, click Verified (2–3 minutes)
Something needs judgmentA text message: which deal, what's stuck, a linkTap the link, read the situation, click Approve or Reject with a note
No response after a few hoursA reminder text; if a deadline gets close, the deal freezes itself and texts you urgentlyA stuck deal can never quietly miss a deadline
WeeklyA digest email: every deal's status, plus a few random auto-approvals to double-checkSkim it; if a rule is behaving badly, fix the rulebook
Wire instructionsA task that only a person can completeCall the known number, verify verbally, mark done

Over time your job shifts from clicking approvals to writing the rules — every time you handle something the rulebook didn't cover, that decision can become a new rule so the Manager handles it next time.

How Approving This Blueprint Works (no GitHub experience needed)

This website's pages live in a shared online folder (GitHub). When we want to change the design, you get a link showing the exact change — old wording and new wording side by side, like tracked changes in Word.

We propose a change→ You get a link showing exactly what would change→ You comment or click Approve→ The website updates itself

Nothing gets built into the real system until its page here is approved. If it's not on this site, it doesn't exist.

TermWhat it means

n8nThe software the workers are built in — think of it as the office building they work in
