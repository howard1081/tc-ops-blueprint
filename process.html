Process Model

The contract-to-close workflow, from the original brainstorm (day buckets) converted to event-driven phases. Day ranges are shown as the typical timeline; phase transitions are triggered by events, not day counts.

Why events, not days: Tennessee deadlines are event-driven — delivery of a Repair Proposal (RF654) ends the inspection period early and starts the resolution period; a 21-day cash closing and a 45-day financed closing pass through the same phases at different speeds. Day buckets remain the human mental model; the system tracks each deal's actual contract dates.

Phase 1 — Intake (Day 1)

  • Executed contract arrives by email → parsed into the deal record (parties, price, earnest money, all contract dates, county, board addenda).
  • Cross-check against MLS data; discrepancies flagged.
  • HUMAN GATE: extracted contract data must be verified by a person before anything downstream runs — a bad date here poisons every deadline after it.
  • On release: deal record created, document checklist instantiated (buyer/seller/cash/financed variant), deadline calendar computed, file-opened emails sent, SkySlope file created, storage folder created.
  • Rolodex linked: our agent, co-op agent, client(s), lender, title company or closing attorney (auto-suggested by county).

Phase 2 — Contingencies (Days 3–14)

Days 3–5 — Money & lender confirmation

  • Earnest money: chase receipt, send agent a copy, verify holder matches contract.
  • Lender follow-up: loan application confirmed complete; appraisal ordered.
  • Inspection scheduling confirmed; calendar reminder to agent (inspection branch is judgment-heavy — human-owned, system-reminded).

Days 6–14 — Inspection, repairs, appraisal, title

  • Client update email: status of everything + local utility contact info (auto-filled by county from the Utilities index).
  • Buyer repair requests → drafted onto Repair Proposal (RF654).
  • HUMAN GATE: our agent approval click required before the proposal is sent to the co-op side. RF654 delivery is logged — it ends the inspection period and starts the resolution period clock.
  • Resolution tracking: executed Repair Amendment (RF655), written as-is acceptance (RF656), or extension — one must exist by the resolution deadline or the contract auto-terminates.
  • Lender: confirm okay to proceed with appraisal.
  • HUMAN GATE: any extension or amendment request needs agent approval before routing.
  • Day-14 checkpoint: check in with title company / closing attorney (title commitment status).

Phase 3 — Closing Prep (Days 15–25)

  • Lender monitoring: if lender is communicating, stay out of the way; if silent, check in — appraisal due date, underwriting status, clear-to-close.
  • Client summary email → propose closing time → book with title/attorney once client confirms.
  • Amendments in this window (price reduction, seller credit, closing date change RF657) route through the agent-approval gate.
  • Preliminary ALTA / settlement statement received → staged for review.
  • HUMAN GATE: ALTA/settlement statement reviewed line-by-line (prorations, commissions, credits) and approved before the client sees it.
  • Buyer email: closing date/time/location, what to bring, final walkthrough reminder, buyer's ALTA, any final disclosure updates.
  • HUMAN GATE (hard, non-automatable): wire instructions are never trusted from email — verbal verification only. The system reminds; a human verifies.

Phase 4 — Closing Day (Days 26–30)

  • Request final signed documents from title/attorney.
  • Upload complete document set to SkySlope; verify every checklist item is satisfied.
  • Confirm recording / funding before the file is marked closed.

Phase 5 — Post-Close

  • SkySlope final audit pass → broker approval.
  • Client email: congratulations, review request, referral ask (branded template).
  • CRM updated; deal archived to the indexed retention archive (TN REC: 3 years, retrievable within 24 hours).

Source: Original Brainstorm Notes

This model is built from the handwritten brainstorming session (5 pages, transcribed 2026-08-18). Key elements carried forward:

  • Day buckets 1 / 3–5 / 6–14 / 15–25 / closing → the five phases above.
  • "Approval click" before repair proposals cross to the other side → formalized as human gates.
  • Rolodex of lenders (agent-updatable), utilities index by locale, spreadsheet as deal tracker → the database schema.
  • SkySlope as the compliance back end ("archaic solution") — filed to, not driven by.
  • Post-close reviews/referrals email + CRM update.
  • Templates carry a design element: logo, color palette, disclaimers.

Open items from the notes still to resolve: storage provider (Dropbox vs. other), the "Jo" role assignments, and the detailed extension-request flow.
